Symptoms

What your refrigerator is telling you

The problems I see most, what usually causes them, and how I fix them.

Fridge warm, freezer still cold

Usual cause: Failed evaporator fan, defrost heater or defrost thermostat. Frost builds on the coil and air stops moving.

What I do: I clear the frost, test the defrost circuit and replace the failed part. Same-day in most cases.

Both fridge and freezer warm

Usual cause: Dirty condenser coils, a dead condenser fan, a failed start relay or, less often, the compressor.

What I do: Coils and fans are quick. If it is the compressor I tell you the real cost before anything is touched.

Runs all the time, loud or clicking

Usual cause: Condenser coils packed with dust or pet hair, or a compressor start relay clicking on and off.

What I do: Coil cleaning and a relay are both under an hour.

Water on the floor or inside

Usual cause: Clogged defrost drain, cracked water line to the ice maker, or a failed door gasket.

What I do: Drain clears, line and gasket replacements done on the spot.

Ice maker stopped

Usual cause: Water inlet valve, frozen fill tube or the ice maker assembly itself.

What I do: See the ice maker page, or call and I will sort it in the same visit.

How I work

Diagnose first. Price before parts.

I start with the coils and fans because that is where most refrigerator problems live, then check the defrost system and controls. You get a clear price before any part goes in. If the fix is not worth it, I say so.

Repair or replace?

A refrigerator under about 12 years old with a fan, coil, defrost or relay problem is almost always worth repairing. Sealed-system failures on a budget unit past ten years usually are not, and I will tell you that on the phone if the symptoms point that way.
